30 Affirmations for Overthinking: Calm a Racing Mind

Overthinking usually takes one of a few shapes: thoughts that race, worries about what might happen, conversations you keep replaying, and decisions you can't stop weighing. This page has six affirmations for each, plus a set for coming back to the present, chosen from Bloom's library of 1,500+ affirmations. They're short on purpose: a busy mind won't hold on to a long sentence.

Racing thoughts

For when your mind is running ten tabs at once and none of them will close.

  1. I trade racing thoughts for one calm moment.
  2. I do not have to react to every thought.
  3. I am calmer than my busiest thoughts.
  4. I understand my thoughts settle like dust after motion.
  5. I trust my thoughts drift by like clouds.
  6. I give my mind one simple focus.

Worry and what-ifs

For the worst-case stories your mind writes about things that haven't happened.

  1. I notice my worry and let it pass without dwelling on it.
  2. I believe worry can visit, but it does not move in.
  3. I notice catastrophizing and return to facts.
  4. I feel one slow pause interrupts the spiral.
  5. I trade the spiral for the present.
  6. I am steadier than the doubt that visits me.

Replaying conversations

For the thing you said hours ago that you're still rewinding.

  1. I release the conversation I keep replaying.
  2. Most people aren't judging me — they're focused on themselves.
  3. I trust the present loosens the past's grip.
  4. I choose curiosity over judgment, about myself.
  5. I trust I'm doing better than I think.
  6. I notice today is different from the last hard day.

Decisions

For when weighing every option has become its own problem.

  1. I decide and trust I can adjust later.
  2. I am allowed to change my mind.
  3. I trust the decisions my calm mind makes.
  4. I trust my judgment more than my fear.
  5. I release urgency that leads to poor decisions.
  6. I am proud of my effort, not perfection.

Back to right now

Small ways out of your head and into the room you're in.

  1. I notice five calm things around me.
  2. I believe the present can be small and manageable.
  3. I feel one honest, calm moment replaces ten anxious thoughts.
  4. I walk outside to reset my mind.
  5. I let my mind be calm, and that is okay.
  6. I give hard emotions room, not control.

How to use them

Find the section that matches the loop you're in and pick one line. Say it slowly, once, then do one small physical thing: a breath out, a sip of water, a look out the window. The aim isn't to argue with the thought. It's to stop feeding it for a moment.

Affirmations can help interrupt a thought loop, but they are not a treatment for anxiety or any other condition. If overthinking is affecting your sleep, work or relationships, talking to a doctor or therapist can help.
