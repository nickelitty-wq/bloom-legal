The Best Affirmation Apps for iPhone (2026)

Updated November 2026

Affirmation apps all promise a more positive mindset, but they work in different ways. Some send reminders, some have you record your own voice, some are part of bigger meditation apps. Here is how the main options compare and who each one suits.

We make Bloom, so weigh our opinion accordingly. We have tried to be fair and to say when another app is the better choice.

What to look for

  • Believable affirmations. Short lines you can actually say to yourself beat grand claims you don't believe.
  • A habit, not a feed. Reminders at times you choose matter more than a giant library you scroll once.
  • Fit with your beliefs. Some people want faith-based language, others want none. Check before you subscribe.
  • Price you'll keep paying. Affirmations work through repetition over months, so the yearly price matters.

1. Bloom: best for personalized daily affirmations

Bloom asks what you are working on during a short setup, such as anxiety, confidence or self-love, then builds a daily mix from 1,500+ affirmations across 10 categories and sends them as reminders. Its most distinctive feature is wording: you choose secular, spiritual or faith-based language, and every affirmation is shown in that style. It also has home and lock screen widgets, focus sessions (3 days to a month on one area), favorites and shareable affirmation cards.

Good fit if: you want something simple that adapts to you, or you have struggled with apps whose tone didn't match your beliefs. Not a fit if: you use Android, or you want to record affirmations in your own voice.

2. I am: best-known reminder app

I am by Monkey Taps has been around since 2014 and has hundreds of thousands of App Store ratings. It sends affirmation reminders through the day and lets you customize backgrounds and themes. It is on both iPhone and Android.

Good fit if: you want the most established option and use Android. Watch out for: it is one of the more expensive options at full price.

3. ThinkUp: best for your own voice

ThinkUp takes a different approach: you pick affirmations (or write your own) and record them in your own voice, then listen with background music. The free version lets you record a few affirmations; Premium unlocks unlimited recordings and more music.

Good fit if: you like listening more than reading, for example on a walk or before sleep. Watch out for: recording takes some setup, and some people find hearing their own voice awkward at first.

4. Meditation apps (Calm, Headspace)

If affirmations are one part of a bigger mental-wellness routine, a meditation app may cover more ground. They are not focused on affirmations, but they include guided sessions on self-talk, sleep and stress.

Questions

What is the best affirmation app for iPhone?

It depends on how you like to practice. I am is the most established reminder-style app. ThinkUp is the pick if you want to hear affirmations in your own recorded voice. Bloom is a good fit if you want affirmations personalized to your goals, in secular, spiritual or faith-based wording, at a lower price than I am.

Is there a free affirmation app?

Bloom, I am and ThinkUp are all free to download, but each keeps most features behind a subscription. If you want something fully free, a list of affirmations in your Notes app plus a daily reminder works surprisingly well.

Which affirmation app is best for anxiety?

Look for an app with short, grounded affirmations and gentle reminders rather than big manifestation claims. Bloom has a dedicated anxiety and calm category; you can read 30 of those affirmations free on this site. Affirmations are not a treatment for an anxiety disorder, so talk to a professional if anxiety is affecting your daily life.

Do I need an app for affirmations?

No. An app mainly helps with consistency: it reminds you, gives you fresh lines and keeps the habit going. If you already have a routine that works, a notebook is fine.
